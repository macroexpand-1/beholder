Solving a terminal palette for one pair of eyes

This is the reasoning behind the light theme in this repo. The short version: none of the colors were chosen. They were solved, against four constraints that fight each other, and the interesting part is how they fight.

The constraints

ConditionWhat it demands
Myopia −1.25 + astigmatismResidual blur even when corrected. Blur scales with pupil size.
Presbyopia (reading add)Less accommodation available; depth of focus matters more.
Red/green color vision deficiencyThe hue circle collapses to one axis. Half of normal color coding is unavailable.
Long working hoursGlare and absolute luminance are real costs, not aesthetics.

1. Why light, in diopters

The usual dark-vs-light argument is taste. This one isn't. Pupil diameter is driven by field luminance, and geometric blur from residual defocus scales linearly with pupil diameter.

Using the Stanley & Davies (1995) pupil model as unified by Watson & Yellott (2012), at a 150-nit display white over a ~30°×18° field:

Fieldcd/m²PupilBlur @ 0.50 D
TokyoNight Storm #24283b3.35.42 mm9.3 arcmin
Warm paper #f2e9d8123.23.44 mm5.9 arcmin

A 37% reduction in retinal blur from the same uncorrected error, and the astigmatic blur ellipse shrinks by the same factor. This is the one change that helps defocus, astigmatism and presbyopia simultaneously. It also retires the halation workaround the dark theme needed: dark-on-light spreads dark into light, which does not bloom.

2. Why not white

Pure white constricts the pupil to 3.35 mm (only 0.09 mm past the warm paper) while raising field luminance 22%. Essentially all of the optical benefit is already captured at L* 92.6. The rest is glare you'd be paying for nothing.

3. Why warm

The eye is not corrected for chromatic aberration. Thibos' chromatic-eye model (1992) gives the defocus of each wavelength relative to 555 nm:

WavelengthDefocus vs 555 nm
450 nm (deep blue)−0.83 D
490 nm (cyan)−0.44 D
610 nm (red)+0.26 D

Blue is roughly three times further out of focus than red, and the total spread across the visible band is over a diopter; you cannot focus both ends at once. Weighting the background long makes it the best-focused thing on screen. It also has two knock-on rules: never put body text in saturated blue, and where blue must be used, give it extra luminance contrast, since the chromatic edge arrives blurred and only the luminance edge survives.

4. The colorblind constraint, and one correction most tools miss

Candidate colors are run through the Viénot, Brettel & Mollon (1999) dichromat simulation and scored on their worst-case separation across normal, protanope and deuteranope vision. Taking the minimum rather than the mean is the whole point: a pair only counts as distinguishable if it survives every way of seeing it.

WCAG overstates reds. The contrast formula's luminance coefficients encode a normal observer's luminous efficiency. A saturated red that measures 4.5:1 reads substantially darker to a protanope. Every color here is checked against all three observers, not just the default one, which is why some reds ended up darker than the naive calculation would require.

The structural consequence is severe and worth stating plainly: under dichromacy the hue circle collapses onto a single blue–yellow line. Six syntax categories are therefore competing for room along one axis plus lightness. Some faces must differ by lightness alone. That is forced by the mathematics, not a failure of the palette.

5. The finding that changed the design

On a light background you cannot maximize contrast and colorfulness at once: sRGB's most chromatic colors sit at middle lightness, and demanding contrast drags you away from there. Measuring the average maximum CIELAB chroma achievable over 24 hues:

Backgroundat AA 4.5:1at 7:1
Dark #24283b82.346.3
Warm paper #f2e9d872.860.4
White77.566.0

The intuition that dark backgrounds carry bolder color is correct at AA, and it inverts at high contrast. At 7:1 the paper carries 30% more chroma than the dark field, because bright saturated colors simply are not bright enough to hold contrast against black. At the contrast these eyes need, light is the better substrate for vivid color.

Two practical consequences. Lightening the paper is not a useful lever: going all the way to white buys ~6% chroma for 22% more glare. The contrast floor is the lever: dropping 7:1 to 4.5:1 buys 20% more chroma.

6. Two solving modes

The original mistake was maximizing contrast everywhere. Syntax keywords sat at 11.06:1 against a 4.5 requirement: six wasted stops, paid for in color, which is exactly why the first light version read as uniformly dark and lifeless.

  • Read for minutes (body text, syntax) → solve for contrast, 4.5–11:1. Correctness first.
  • Glanced at (prompt, diffs, status) → solve for chroma subject to a floor. Everything above the floor is contrast you are not using; spend it as color.

Re-solving this way roughly tripled the chroma on the prompt directory and quadrupled it on added diff lines, changing no ratio that mattered.

Hue assignment by token frequency

Because dichromacy flattens hue to one axis, the richest and most separable bands should go to the tokens you see most. Keywords therefore take violet rather than the customary amber, and types take the olive slot. Swapping those two was worth +302% chroma on keywords and moved the solution from 97% to 100% of its separation targets.

Comments are a neutral gray, not a warm one: a warm comment and a green string both land on olive under deuteranope simulation, which had capped strings at +12% chroma. Neutralizing the comment freed the entire green band and strings gained +177%.

9. Regenerating

./color/solve-palette.lisp verify   # re-measure everything deployed
./color/solve-palette.lisp syntax   # doom/config.el font-lock faces
./color/solve-palette.lisp prompt   # starship [palettes.paper]
./color/solve-palette.lisp diff     # git [color "diff"]

./color/pupil.lisp                  # pupil size and retinal blur per field
./color/lca.lisp                    # chromatic aberration per wavelength
./color/tradeoff.lisp               # chroma available per background

Run verify after any palette edit; it exits non-zero if anything falls below AA for any of the three observers. The constraints interact, so nudging one hex by hand tends to break a pair somewhere else; change the constraints and re-solve instead.
